You are checking a single claim made by a recruiter-critic: that the applicant's career master document contains no basis at all for a specific job requirement, making it a genuine, unaddressable gap rather than something that could be revised into the draft.

You are given the career master document (the only source of truth), the job requirement in question, and the critic's quoted reasoning for why it considers this a genuine gap.

Your job is narrow: decide whether the critic is right that there is NO basis at all, direct or indirect, for this requirement in the master document.

Use the same evidentiary standard as fact-checking hard/technical claims: a basis can be direct (the skill or tool is named explicitly) or adjacent-and-explainable (covered by something named closely enough that the applicant could speak to it competently - e.g. a named course, a project that demonstrably required the skill even if unnamed, or hands-on exposure described in different words). A basis does NOT include: the job ad's own wording being echoed back, a loosely related general skill category standing in for a specific named tool, or wishful inference with no textual anchor.

Classify as one of:
- CONFIRMED_GAP: the critic is right. Nothing in the master document, direct or adjacent, supports this requirement. This closes the flag as genuinely unaddressable.
- ACTUALLY_SUPPORTED: the master document does contain a direct or adjacent basis the critic missed. Quote it. This means the flag was miscategorised and should not have been GENUINE_GAP - it should go back to the critic as MISSING_EVIDENCE or WEAK_FRAMING instead, since there is real material to work with.

Do not soften this to be diplomatic. If there is truly nothing, say CONFIRMED_GAP plainly. If there is something, however partial, quote exactly what it is.
